# The largest natural pyramid in the world: mystery and guide to Cerro Tusa

Rising with arresting mathematical precision above the coffee plantations of southwestern Antioquia stands one of the most astonishing geological wonders on Earth.

It was not engineered by pharaohs nor sculpted by human hands. It was carved by the ancient volcanic forces of the planet: Cerro Tusa, celebrated worldwide as **the largest natural pyramid in the world**.

With a summit at **1,950 meters (6,400 feet) above sea level** and a sheer vertical relief of nearly 600 meters, this monumental peak is both an archaeological sanctuary and a coveted mountaineering milestone.

---

## 1. Geological genesis: an ancient volcanic plug

To comprehend its geometric symmetry, we must journey millions of years back to the Tertiary period.

Cerro Tusa is not a standard folded mountain range; it is a **volcanic neck or plug**—dense andesitic magma that cooled and solidified within the vent of an extinct volcano.

Across geological epochs, torrential rains and winds from the Cauca River canyon eroded the softer surrounding sedimentary layers. This exposed the resilient tetrahedral core, casting clean triangular shadows over the valley as the sun sets over the Andes.

Geologists and international travelers travel to Colombia drawn by its uncanny resemblance to the pyramids of Giza or Teotihuacan, yet on a much grander natural scale and cloaked entirely in emerald cloud forest and high-altitude grasses.

## 2. The Zenúfana sanctuary: sacred archaeology and mythology

Centuries before European explorers arrived, the valley of Venecia was home to the **Zenúfana people**, an advanced Carib-speaking indigenous culture who revered the mountain as the pillar uniting heaven and earth.

To the Zenúfanas, the pyramid was the dwelling place of their tutelary deities:

- **The Face of the Goddess:** Along the western rock face, the morning sun casts shadows outlining a giant ancestral face, venerated as a deity of fertility and harvest.
- **The Offering Altar:** A carved stone platform discovered along the mountain slopes, historically used for celestial rituals during solstices and equinoxes.
- **Sacred Petroglyphs:** Pre-Columbian basalt boulders etched with spirals, anthropomorphic figures, and solar maps, now protected along the ecological park trails.

## 3. The summit scramble: advanced mountain trekking

Reaching the summit of Cerro Tusa is one of the most respected trekking feats in Colombia.

While its absolute elevation is moderate, the climb presents relentless verticality:
- **Elevation gain:** ~600 vertical meters over just 2.5 kilometers of trail.
- **Incline:** Sustained sections exceeding 70% gradient, requiring hand-over-foot scrambling on roots, anchored ropes, and bare rock faces.
- **Estimated duration:** 4 to 5 hours round trip.
- **Difficulty rating:** Level 5 (Advanced / Demanding).

Climbing must be conducted with certified native mountain guides, such as our trusted partners at **Al Parche Venecia**.

> [!tip]
> **Secret Tip • La Juana:** If you want to experience the sacred archaeology without tackling the demanding summit climb, view the western face between 6:30 a.m. and 7:15 a.m. During this brief window, low morning sunbeams highlight the rock-carved Face of the Goddess with striking clarity.

An early morning departure avoids the intense midday equatorial heat and ensures you reach the top before afternoon clouds form. At the pinnacle, hikers enjoy an awe-inspiring 360-degree panorama spanning the Farallones del Citará and the Cauca River canyon.

## Monument technical and geological data

- **Geographical coordinates:** 5°58′02″N 75°45′40″W.
- **Geological type:** Late Miocene andesitic and dacitic volcanic neck.
- **Summit elevation:** 1,950 meters (6,400 feet) above sea level.
- **Vertical prominence:** 600 meters of freestanding relief from the valley floor.
- **Average slope:** 65% to 75% on the eastern face, among the steepest climbable ridges in South America.
