# Glamping Cerro Tusa: the complete guide to sleeping by the natural pyramid

The first time you see Cerro Tusa at sunset, you understand why the Zenúfanas called it sacred. The light lingers on the slopes, the green turns copper, and the mountain looks like it is glowing from inside. No photograph does it justice.

If you are reading this, you probably typed "glamping cerro tusa" into Google. Not a coincidence. Every month more than a hundred people in Colombia run that exact search. They want to sleep close to the mountain, wake up to the view, and live a glamping experience that mixes comfort with nature, without having to pitch a single tent.

This article is for you. Below I walk through the options that actually exist for staying in front of Cerro Tusa and why, if you will let me brag a little, none of them beats La Juana.

## What is glamping at Cerro Tusa and why is everyone asking about it?

Glamping, short for glamorous camping, promises the camping experience without the downsides: a real bed, electricity, a private bathroom, and sometimes even a hot tub. Instead of sleeping on the ground, you sleep in a dome, a glass cabin, or a safari tent with the landscape as your wall.

Cerro Tusa is the perfect backdrop for it. Two hours from Medellín, in the municipality of Venecia, Antioquia, rises what geologists call the largest natural pyramid in the world. A volcanic formation 1,950 meters tall that stands alone in the valley, with nothing around it to steal the spotlight.

The search for glamping Cerro Tusa keeps growing for a simple reason: there are not many places to stay with this view. And the ones that exist book up fast.

If you came looking for a glamping with a view of Cerro Tusa, you are in the right place. The natural viewpoint this mountain offers is hard to beat in Antioquia. From its slopes, you can see the whole southwest of the department spread out like a map.

## Glamping options near Cerro Tusa

Before recommending anything, I did the fieldwork. Several places in the area advertise themselves as glamping:

**Cerro Tusa Springs:** cabins and suites in the Villa Luz village, less than two kilometers from the mountain. One of the closest options to the attraction.

**Los Pinos Finca Hotel:** domes and cabins in Barbosa with adventure activities (ATVs, paragliding, waterfalls). About 45 minutes from Medellín, but with no view of the mountain.

**Aldea de Los Pioneros:** simple lodging in Venecia with natural pools, booked through WhatsApp and Instagram.

**Napü Ecohotel and Glamping Nubes de Jardín:** domes with a view, a bonfire, and an outdoor hot tub in Jardín, about an hour and a half from the mountain. Pretty, but far from the main attraction.

These are valid options for one or two people who want a night in a dome. But they all share the same limit: they are small, individual units. None of them gives you the whole property, room for an entire group, or a team that handles everything. That is where La Juana comes in. If you are planning a full trip, this rundown of [things to do in Venecia, Antioquia](/en/blog/cerro-tusa/things-to-do-venecia-antioquia) covers the mountain, the murals, and the town itself.
