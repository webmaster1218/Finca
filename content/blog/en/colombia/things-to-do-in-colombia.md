# Things to do in Colombia: complete travel guide beyond the standard trail

Colombia has evolved far beyond an up-and-coming destination into one of the most vibrant, diverse, and hospitable countries on the planet. Situated at the northwestern crossroads of South America, where the Pacific Ocean, Caribbean Sea, Amazon rainforest, and three towering cordilleras of the Andes converge, this nation captivates travelers with its kaleidoscopic variety of ecosystems, music, and culinary traditions.

Yet many international visitors fall into a predictable tourist circuit: a brief museum stop in Bogotá, two days in Cartagena's walled city, and an urban weekend in Medellín. When asking **what to do in Colombia** to touch the authentic soul of the country, the answer lies in venturing into the emerald heart of the Andean coffee mountains, where life moves to agrarian rhythms and warmth is offered with an open heart.

---

## 1. Megadiverse geography: one country, infinite landscapes

Few places on Earth pack so many distinct worlds into a single nation:
- **The Caribbean Coast:** White sand beaches, coral reefs, and the Spanish colonial ramparts of Cartagena and Santa Marta.
- **The Pacific Biogeographic Realm:** Pristine tropical rainforests where humpback whales give birth in warm coastal waters.
- **The Amazon and Eastern Plains:** Endless savannas and primary jungle holding the planet's richest freshwater biodiversity.
- **The Andean Coffee Region:** The economic and cultural cradle of the nation, where rural families sculpted vertical mountain slopes to cultivate the world's smoothest Arabica coffee.

Within the Andes, the department of Antioquia serves as the engine of this tradition. Learn more in our regional guide on [things to do in Antioquia](/en/blog/antioquia-travel/things-to-do-in-antioquia).

## 2. The UNESCO Coffee Cultural Landscape

Designated a World Heritage site by UNESCO for its outstanding universal value, Colombia's coffee region is far more than farmland; it is an enduring model of harmony between human settlement and dramatic alpine nature:

- **Bahareque Colonial Architecture:** Lofty cedarwood ceilings, shaded wrap-around verandas, and clay tile roofs painted in joyous primary colors.
- **The Muleteer Legacy («Arriería»):** Tales of resilient mountain men and pack mules who carved historical stone trails across steep gorges to connect the country.
- **Single-Origin Specialty Coffee:** Witnessing hand-selected picking of ripe red Arabica cherries, followed by pour-over tastings displaying bright floral, honey, and citrus notes.

In southwestern Antioquia, this cultural landscape reaches its most dramatic mountain relief in towns like Venecia, Jericó, and Jardín. Explore our curated route across [top heritage towns in Antioquia](/en/blog/antioquia-travel/heritage-towns-antioquia).

## 3. Cerro Tusa: the world's largest natural pyramid

One of Colombia's best-kept secrets for international travelers is Cerro Tusa, located in the coffee municipality of Venecia. Celebrated as **the largest natural pyramid in the world**, this 1,950-meter (6,400 ft) volcanic monolith rises in mathematical symmetry above rolling green slopes.

Travelers arriving in this sacred valley discover:
- **Pre-Columbian Zenúfana Heritage:** Ancient petroglyphs, carved stone water channels, and the mythical Offering Altar.
- **Alpine Hiking and Mountaineering:** Family-friendly nature trails within Parque Comfama Cerro Tusa alongside technical ascents to the pyramid summit.
- **Grounding Visual Magnetism:** Waking up every morning facing a natural monument that defies architectural imagination.

Explore this ancient landmark in our deep dive on [the largest natural pyramid in the world](/en/blog/cerro-tusa/largest-natural-pyramid-in-the-world).

## Suggested 2-Week Colombia Travel Itinerary

To balance cosmopolitan culture, mountain heritage, and tropical coasts:
- **Days 1 to 3:** Bogotá (Gold Museum, historic La Candelaria, award-winning dining).
- **Days 4 to 6:** Medellín (Urban transformation, Comuna 13, botanic gardens, vibrant nightlife).
- **Days 7 to 10:** Suroeste Antioquia at La Juana Cerro Tusa (Specialty coffee tours, hiking Cerro Tusa, heritage towns of Jericó and Jardín, pool and jacuzzi relaxation).
- **Days 11 to 14:** Cartagena & Rosario Islands (Colonial fortress walls, Caribbean seafood, and tropical island beaches).

## International Traveler Practical Tips

- **Currency:** Colombian Peso (COP). Credit cards are accepted in cities; carry cash for rural markets.
- **Health & Vaccines:** Venecia's altitude (1,500m) means yellow fever is not endemic; standard travel immunizations apply.
- **Electrical Standards:** Colombia uses Type A and B plugs at 110V/60Hz (same as the United States and Canada).

---

## Practical Guidance: Currency, Tipping, and Local Payments

A seamless travel experience in Colombia relies on practical financial and connectivity preparedness:

- **Currency and ATMs:** Colombia uses the Colombian Peso (COP). Major credit cards (Visa and Mastercard) are widely accepted in hotels, supermarkets, and established restaurants in Medellín. However, carrying small denominations of cash ($10,000, $20,000, and $50,000 COP bills) is essential for local fruit stands, market vendors, and town cafes in Venecia.
- **Tipping Norms (*La Propina*):** In Colombian restaurants, a voluntary 10% tip (*propina voluntaria*) is customarily added to the bill upon verbal confirmation. When renting private luxury estates like La Juana, offering an appreciative tip to the house staff (butler and maids) at check-out is customary and deeply appreciated for exceptional service.
- **Mobile Connectivity and SIM Cards:** Local eSIM cards (Claro, Tigo, or Movistar) provide robust LTE coverage throughout the Medellín-Venecia highway corridor and in town centers, complemented by fast Starlink satellite WiFi at La Juana estate.
