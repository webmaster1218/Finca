# Family vacation in Medellín with kids: a practical guide

Planning a family vacation in Medellín with kids usually starts with a list of hotels and ends in a tired negotiation: this one can't fit a family of five, that one frowns at children in the pool, breakfast doesn't start until nine and the youngest eats at seven. When you travel with the whole household, a hotel room runs out of air fast.

This guide starts from a simple idea, learned from families who come to the Suroeste of Antioquia year after year: with kids, where you stay is not a detail of the trip. It is the trip. If the place they sleep is also the place they play, eat and crash, everything else gets easy.

---

## Why a whole farmhouse changes a family vacation in Medellín with kids

A hotel slices the day into pieces: breakfast in the lobby, pool with a schedule, naps fought against hallway noise. A private farmhouse puts everything under one roof and on one piece of land. The kids wake up already on vacation; nobody has to load them into a car to find fun.

In the Suroeste, two and a half hours from the city, that format pays off even more. The weather stays mild year-round, distances are short, and the landscape does the entertaining on its own. And there is something no hotel room offers: kids running barefoot on the grass while the grown-ups finish their coffee facing the mountain.

La Juana Cerro Tusa works exactly like that. The estate rents as a whole, sleeping up to 16 people in 6 bedrooms, and the house team — a butler and two housekeepers — comes included. For an extended family traveling with grandparents and cousins, that means everyone under one roof without sharing spaces with strangers. The full experience, jacuzzi, steam room and fire pit included, is described in our piece on the [luxury farmhouse in the Suroeste of Antioquia](/en/blog/luxury-fincas/finca-la-juana-luxury-experience).

## What to look for: a checklist for parents

Before booking anywhere with children, five things deserve a hard look. They have saved us more than once:

- **Exclusive use of the property.** Sharing a pool or dining room with other guests multiplies the supervision you need and splits the parents' attention.
- **Cooking service included.** Cooking on vacation is just working in a different kitchen. Having someone else handle breakfast changes the mood of the whole group.
- **Outdoor space inside the grounds.** Gardens, a patio, a stretch of grass: anywhere children can move without crossing a road.
- **Activities nearby, not one fixed program.** A place that has everything is fine, but the surroundings should offer one or two outings for the week.
- **A manageable distance from Medellín.** With kids, every hour in the car counts. Under three hours of travel, the drive doesn't burn the first day.

La Juana checks all five: whole-house rental, house staff, wide gardens, [Parque Comfama Cerro Tusa five minutes away](/en/blog/cerro-tusa/things-to-do-venecia-antioquia), and a two-and-a-half-hour drive from the city.

## When to go: the family calendar

School calendars rule family travel, and the Suroeste receives visitors differently depending on the season. Worth thinking about before you pick a date:

**June and July** are the peak weeks for families with school-age children. The weather cooperates and the region fills with life, but it's also when the farmhouse books out first. If these are your weeks, reserve with real lead time, not a month out.

**December and January** have their own pull: Colombia's lights season, the longest school break of the year, and end-of-year mountain weather that calls for a fire pit and hot chocolate. Large families gathering cousins and grandparents favor these dates.

**Easter week** folds the holiday and the school break into one window, and the Suroeste is a classic destination for it. The short distance from Medellín makes it work even if you only have four days.

**Between seasons** — March, August, September, October — is the quiet opportunity: the same gardens, the same pool, the same mountain, with fewer visitors around and easier pick of dates. For families with children too young for school, or parents with flexible schedules, it is usually the best mix of weather, price and calm.

## A plan by age: what each kid actually does

The point of a farmhouse is that it doesn't force one rhythm. Here is roughly what it looks like by age:

### Ages 0 to 5

Pool time with floaties in the sunny hours, long naps under the trees, afternoons in the garden. At this age what they need is soft space and rested adults; the butler and housekeepers, who know the property by heart, become allies for taking turns on supervision. Our account of a [sunny day in Antioquia](/en/blog/antioquia-travel/day-by-the-pool-antioquia) gives a feel for the pace.

### Ages 6 to 11

The golden age of the farmhouse. Horseback rides on the trails of Venecia, treasure hunts between coffee plants, the evening fire pit with stories and marshmallows. This is also when the first short guided hike in the neighboring park starts to make sense.

### Ages 12 and up

Teenagers get their challenge: the Cerro Tusa ascent, four to five guided hours to the top of the natural pyramid, passing terraces of indigenous origin on the way up. Coming back with that photo beats any screen. ## What a day at the farmhouse looks like

So it doesn't stay in the imagination, here is a regular day for a family at La Juana:

The day begins with freshly brewed local coffee, because the housekeepers have it ready before the first person comes downstairs. Breakfast lands on the table without anyone ordering: arepas, eggs, fruit from the region. Mid-morning is pool time while the sun climbs, and toward noon the smell of firewood announces the barbecue on the terrace.

The afternoon splits between naps for the little ones, reading for the grown-ups in the garden chairs, and — if there's energy left — a short outing to the park or a horseback ride. When the sun drops behind Cerro Tusa, the fire pit goes up. Nobody asks what time it is.

That rhythm, with no locked itinerary and no hotel schedules, is exactly what families come here for.

## Three common mistakes (and how to dodge them)

- **Over-planning.** Arriving with a daily agenda fights the whole farmhouse format. One or two outings across the week, and let the house set the rest of the pace.
- **Underestimating the last stretch of road.** The highway to the Suroeste is in good shape, but the final blocks are unpaved. No car struggles with it; it just helps to know before you arrive.
- **Leaving the booking late.** Weekends and holiday breaks fill earlier than people expect, especially around school vacations. Two months of lead time secures the date and the full house.
